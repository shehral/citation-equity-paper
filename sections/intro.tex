% ---- Stakes
% src: 18.8 percentage points: R77; notes/NARRATIVE.md 2.1 (checked in the full text, Section 3, 2026-10-24)
Answer engines, systems that answer a question in prose and cite web sources, now decide which pages a reader sees cited.
In a field experiment, a search page with only AI Mode cut click-through by 18.8 percentage points~\cite{wang2026aisearch}.
Writers respond with generative engine optimization (GEO): advice and products for getting a page cited.

Most of that advice is untested.
Its best-known figure, gains of up to 40\%, comes from one setup with five sources in one model's prompt~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}; a survey of 45 studies rejects it as a general claim~\cite{martinez2026survey}, and adding statistics lowered citation rank in 19 of 24 C-SEO Bench settings~\cite{puerto2025cseo}.
Of 24 commercial products whose public pages we read, most sell brand tracking and seven also rewrite the text,\footnote{For example \url{https://www.evertune.ai/pricing} and \url{https://writesonic.com/pricing}, opened 24 October 2026.} yet writers feel less ownership of text an AI writes for them~\cite{draxler2024ghostwriter,dhillon2024scaffolding}.

We built that advice into a writing tool and tested each of its ten checks on its own, beside candidate checks of our own.
The one candidate that helped became its eleventh.
An open model or a fixed rule made one check's edit to real web pages, against a same-length paraphrase of the same passage or the unedited page, and open models answered from fixed sources (80 GEO-bench queries, with 60 from C-SEO Bench as a replication set).
Every test was planned in advance.
Only the reader's own words near the top of the page clearly raised citation (Figure~\ref{fig:result}a).
A follow-up found that the heading itself added nothing.
A last test asked what a writer can know.
The gain held on 180 queries the first test never used.
A keyword's two words helped, but less.
A question a local model wrote from the keyword showed no effect larger than 0.03 (Figure~\ref{fig:result}b).

We present \emph{Citation Equity}, an open-source writing tool rebuilt around these results (Figure~\ref{fig:workspace}).\footnote{Live demo: \url{https://shehral.github.io/citation-equity/}. Code (MIT): \url{https://github.com/shehral/citation-equity}. Data: \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Shehral/citation-equity-experiments}. Research page (plans, results, evidence grades): \url{https://shehral.github.io/citation-equity/research/}.\label{fn:links} Video: \url{https://youtu.be/kbU9t3ewVUk}. \owner{Re-record for the question-first tool.}}
It starts from the questions readers ask, counts which ones a draft covers, names the missing words without inserting them, flags settings that keep a page out of answers, and labels the rest of the advice with what our tests found.
The equity is of access, not of exposure in rankings: the tool is free, open and offline, so the evidence is not reserved for brands that pay for tracking tools.
Our contributions are (1)~a controlled test of eleven common GEO checks, with placebos, a replication set, an editor swap, and tests on new queries and on questions a writer can guess; (2)~the finding that vocabulary, not formatting, moved citation; and (3)~an open, offline writing tool rebuilt from that finding, with a live demo, code and data.
