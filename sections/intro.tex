% ---- Stakes
% src: 18.8 percentage points: R77; notes/NARRATIVE.md 2.1 (checked in the full text, Section 3, 2026-10-24)
Answer engines, systems that answer a question in prose and cite web sources, now decide which pages a reader sees cited.
In a field experiment, a search page with only AI Mode cut click-through by 18.8 percentage points~\cite{wang2026aisearch}.
Writers respond with generative engine optimization (GEO): advice and products for getting a page cited.

GEO-bench and C-SEO Bench tested content rewrites (statistics, quotes, citations; keyword stuffing in GEO-bench)~\cite{aggarwal2024geo,puerto2025cseo}; the structural advice (question headings, lists, tables, an answer first) had not been tested one change at a time against a same-length placebo.
The best-known figure, gains of up to 40\%, comes chiefly from one setup with five sources in one model's prompt~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}; a survey of 45 studies rejects it as a general claim~\cite{martinez2026survey}, and adding statistics lowered citation rank in 19 of 24 C-SEO Bench settings~\cite{puerto2025cseo}.
Of 24 commercial products whose public pages we read, most sell brand tracking and seven also rewrite the text,\footnote{For example \url{https://www.evertune.ai/pricing} and \url{https://writesonic.com/pricing}, opened 24 October 2026.} yet writers feel less ownership of text an AI writes for them~\cite{draxler2024ghostwriter}.

We built that advice into a writing tool and tested each of its ten checks on its own, beside candidate checks of our own; the one candidate that helped became its eleventh.
For each check, an open model or a fixed rule made that one edit to real web pages, and open models answered from fixed sources (80 GEO-bench queries, with 60 from C-SEO Bench as a replication set); every test was planned in advance.
Only the reader's own words near the top of the page clearly raised citation (Figure~\ref{fig:result}a).
A follow-up found that the heading itself added nothing.
A last test asked what a writer can know: on the 180 new queries (115 where the edit applied) the gain held, a keyword's two words helped, but less, and a question a local model wrote from the keyword showed no effect larger than 0.03 (Figure~\ref{fig:result}b).

We present \emph{Citation Equity}, an open-source writing tool rebuilt around these results (Figure~\ref{fig:workspace}).\footnote{Live demo: \url{https://shehral.github.io/citation-equity/}. Code (MIT): \url{https://github.com/shehral/citation-equity}. Data (all tests): \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Shehral/citation-equity-experiments}. Research page: \url{https://shehral.github.io/citation-equity/research/}.\label{fn:links} Video: \url{https://youtu.be/kbU9t3ewVUk}. \owner{Re-record for the question-first tool.}}
It starts from the questions readers ask, names the words a draft lacks near the top without inserting them, and labels the rest of the advice with what our tests found.
The equity is of access, not of rankings: the tool is free, open and offline, so the evidence is not reserved for brands that pay for tracking tools.
Our contributions are (1)~a controlled test of ten GEO checks and one of our own, with placebos, a replication set, an editor swap, new queries and guessed questions; (2)~the finding that the reader's vocabulary, not any formatting check we could judge, clearly moved citation; and (3)~an open, offline writing tool rebuilt from that finding, with a live demo, code and data.
