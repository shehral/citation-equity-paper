\paragraph{From a score to questions}
The first version averaged the eleven checks into a 0-to-100 checklist score.
On two sample drafts, following every suggestion raised it by 26 to 29 points, while mean citation share on four open models moved by only 1 to 3 points, and Section~\ref{sec:findings} found clear gains for only two checks.
The tool now shows no total anywhere and starts from the reader's questions.

\paragraph{The flow}\label{sec:flow}
Four screens follow the writer's work.
\emph{Your questions} asks ``What will your readers ask?'': the writer types questions, pastes keywords or imports a Search Console or Bing Webmaster export, or loads an example with one click.
\emph{Your draft} leads with ``Questions covered: N of M'' (Figure~\ref{fig:workspace}).
A question is covered when its key words appear in the title, first heading or first paragraph, and a section opens with a plain sentence that uses most of them; the card names the missing words and never inserts them.
Below sit one ready edit and the other checks as collapsed writing notes.
\emph{Can engines reach it?} checks pasted HTML and robots.txt for three blockers (a blocked AI crawler, a noindex rule, text that needs JavaScript), which rest on the engines' own documentation; the other page items are notes.
\emph{Did it change anything?} runs the citation test on the draft before and after, and shows whom live AI answers cite.

\paragraph{Labels, not scores}\label{sec:labels}
Each check carries one fixed label for what our tests found.
A check that helped reads \emph{Helped in our test}, one whose interval sat inside $\pm 0.03$ reads \emph{No effect in our test}, and one that ran without a verdict reads \emph{Unclear}, since \emph{Not tested} would be untrue; \emph{Not tested} is kept for inputs our tests never used, such as questions made from the page itself.
The two question checks read Helped for the reader's own questions; question headings, lists, cited sources and answer first read No effect; the other five read Unclear.
For a question made from a keyword the answer line reads \emph{May help less} and the words line \emph{No effect in our test}, and for a local model's question the answer line reads \emph{No effect in our test}, label words we chose after seeing the results.

\paragraph{Evidence and the rule for nulls}
Published studies are graded apart from our labels, on the Research page, under a strict rule: a null or negative result in any study, even in one subgroup, makes a check \emph{mixed}.
Over the nine sources the tool cites, each opened and read, no draft check is strong or moderate: nine are mixed and two untested (Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}).
A looser rule, defined after the fact, that counts only a study's own no-effect conclusion or a significant harm makes five mixed checks moderate. \owner{Second rater (Ishan Gupta): agreement pending.}

\paragraph{Suggest, never rewrite}\label{sec:suggest}
Comments anchored to spans of text gave writers more agency than a chat interface in a lab study~\cite{lou2025anchoredai}, so every suggestion is anchored to an exact range of the draft and follows the author's edits.
An edit is previewed and applied only if its text is unchanged; every action has Undo, and there is no ``apply all''.
An optional local model can propose wording for an answer, applied only on the author's click.

\paragraph{Architecture and the citation test}\label{sec:bench}
A TypeScript monorepo holds an \emph{engine} of pure check functions (browser and CLI), a \emph{bench} with the citation test, and a \emph{workspace}, a Next.js static export on GitHub Pages with no server; a browser test asserts that no request leaves the origin on load.
For each question the bench retrieves the top 8 of the draft and its competitor pages with BM25, and a local model (\texttt{llama3.2:3b} through Ollama) answers with numbered citations under GEO's prompt~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}, 8 times with shuffled source orders~\cite[Sec.~11.2]{martinez2026survey}.
Reports keep apart retrieval, valid answers, citation and word share beside the chance level, and each says \emph{``It does not predict live AI engines.''}
With a sample's 4 questions the smallest possible $p$ is 0.125, so a laptop run shows a direction at most.
