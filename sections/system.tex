\paragraph{From a score to questions}
The tool shows no total: it starts from the reader's questions, counts which ones a draft covers, and labels every check with what Section~\ref{sec:findings} found (Figure~\ref{fig:workspace}).
It replaces a 0-to-100 checklist score: on two sample drafts, the old tool's suggestions raised that score by 26 to 29 points while the drafts' share of citations among eight sources rose by 1 to 3 points from about 17 (an exploratory run on four open models).

\paragraph{The flow}\label{sec:flow}
\emph{Your questions} asks ``What will your readers ask?'': the writer types questions, pastes keywords, imports a Search Console or Bing Webmaster export, or loads an example.
\emph{Your draft} leads with ``Questions covered: N of M''.
A question is covered when its key words appear in the title, first heading or first paragraph, and a section opens with a plain sentence that uses most of them.
The card names the missing words and never inserts them.
Below sit one ready edit, the \emph{Quick edit}, and the other checks as collapsed writing notes.
\emph{Your site} (``Can engines reach it?'') checks pasted HTML and robots.txt for three blockers (a blocked AI crawler, a noindex rule, text that needs JavaScript), which rest on the engines' own documentation; the other page items are notes.
\emph{Results} (``Did it change anything?'') runs the citation test on the draft before and after, and tallies whom AI answers cite when the writer pastes them.

\paragraph{Labels, not scores}\label{sec:labels}
Each check carries one fixed label for what our tests found: \emph{Helped in our test}; \emph{No effect in our test}, for no effect larger than 0.03; or \emph{Unclear}, for one that ran without a verdict, since \emph{Not tested} would be untrue.
\emph{Not tested} is kept for inputs no test judged, such as questions made from the page itself, only a placebo in our last test.
The two question checks read Helped for the reader's own questions, typed or imported, though our tests used only full questions.
Question headings, lists, cited sources and a direct answer first read No effect; the other five read Unclear.
The \emph{Questions covered} count asks for both measured parts at once, the words up top and an opening sentence of 8 to 40 words that answers, a stricter bar than the question section met (27\% of pages): each part was tested on its own, and the count makes the writer do both, so it is a to-do list built from what helped, not a predictor.
A keyword's question reads \emph{May help less} for its answer and \emph{No effect} for its words, and a local model's question \emph{No effect}, measured with the 31B editor model, not the tool's 3B default; both are rules chosen after the results (Research page, footnote~\ref{fn:links}).

\paragraph{Evidence}
Apart from our labels, the Research page grades each check on the nine studies the tool cites under a strict rule: a null or negative result in any study, even in one subgroup, makes it \emph{mixed}.
By that rule, coded by one author, nine checks are mixed and two untested.

\paragraph{Suggest, never rewrite}\label{sec:suggest}
Comments anchored to spans of text gave writers more agency than a chat interface in a lab study~\cite{lou2025anchoredai}, so every suggestion is anchored to an exact range of the draft and follows the author's edits.
An edit is previewed and applied only if its text is unchanged, every action has Undo, there is no ``apply all'', and an optional local model proposes wording only on the author's click.

\paragraph{Architecture and the citation test}\label{sec:bench}
A TypeScript monorepo holds an \emph{engine} of pure check functions (browser and CLI), a \emph{bench} with the citation test, and a \emph{workspace}, a static export on GitHub Pages with no server; a browser test asserts that no request leaves the origin on load.
For each question the bench retrieves the top 8 of the draft and its competitor pages with BM25, and a local model (\texttt{llama3.2:3b} through Ollama, the default) answers with numbered citations under GEO's prompt~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}, 8 times with shuffled source orders~\cite[Sec.~11.2]{martinez2026survey}.
Reports show the chance level and say \emph{``It does not predict live AI engines.''}
With a sample's 4 questions the smallest possible $p$ is 0.125, so a laptop run shows a direction at most.
