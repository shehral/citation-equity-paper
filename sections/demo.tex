\emph{Station 1, the 90-second loop.}
The visitor clicks \emph{Try it with an example} and sees four reader questions, a draft that covers none of them, and one blocker on the example page.
The ready edit adds a question mark to a heading, common GEO advice, and its chip reads \emph{No effect in our test}; applied through Preview, it leaves the count at 0 of 4.
The visitor then opens the example's recorded before-and-after test beside its chance line; its after draft followed the old checklist.
A live run (64 answers, 15 minutes) starts each session and fills in while visitors watch.
Last, two typed edits, a few of the reader's words in the title and one answering sentence, take the count to 2 of 4 (Figure~\ref{fig:workspace}).

\emph{Station 2, your readers' questions (offline).}
Visitors type their questions or keywords and paste their own paragraph.
They see which questions it covers and which words are missing, and the text never leaves the laptop.

\emph{Station 3, can engines reach your site? (online).}
The command-line \texttt{ce site} checks the visitor's URL for blockers over a phone hotspot.
Offline, pasted HTML and robots.txt run the same checks in the browser, except those that need the live site.
