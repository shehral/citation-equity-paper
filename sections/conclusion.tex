\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:conclusion}
Every measurement uses open models over fixed sources, not live engines, which differ and drift~\cite{martinez2026survey}.
The effects are small, about 0.03 on a 0-to-1 score, or about one rank position over eight answers.
The keyword in our last test is two words of the real query, so it carries part of the query by design; real keywords may share less.
Our first test's queries are those on which the question section first passed, so estimates that include them may run high; the 180 new queries do not have this bias.
An earlier sensitivity test of the bench missed its target: a one-sentence answer edit gained $+0.007$ to $+0.034$ against a $+0.10$ bar the harness could detect.
The tests cover 320 English queries, nearly all edited by one model family, and the harness prompt has not been byte-checked against the bench's.
Next are a comparison with live engines and a study with writers.

\section{Ethics and use of data}\label{sec:ethics}

No human participants took part and no user data was collected.
Sample drafts and competitor pages are invented; C-SEO Bench is downloaded at run time, never redistributed; building competitor pages from URLs respects robots.txt.
% src: CE/README.md line 3 ("it suggests, you decide"); CE/docs/architecture.md lines 84 and 90 (no bulk action; the local model only
%      after a click); FTC review rule and Endorsement Guides: CE/docs/evidence.md lines 494 and 530 (CE 7b10ad3)
On dual use, the tool writes no page text on its own, asks for real and checkable material, and states the FTC's endorsement rules; the bench could test a manipulative edit, but the tool offers none.
% src: CE/experiments/PREREG_E1.md line 18 (GEO-bench source URLs: research use only, never redistributed; only aggregates and

The tests use the public pages GEO-bench lists~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}; their text, and everything built from it, is not redistributed, only URLs, hashes and results.
A future user study will go through IRB review.
