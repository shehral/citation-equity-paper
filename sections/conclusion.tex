\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:conclusion}
Our one-change tests put the page among four fixed sources, so they measure citation, not whether the page is found; with retrieval back in such gains can reverse~\cite{kim2026sageo}, and live engines differ and drift~\cite{martinez2026survey}.
The effects are small, about 0.03 on a 0-to-1 score on which the unedited page averages 0.54, or about one rank position over eight answers.
The keyword in our last test is two words of the real query, so it carries part of the query by design; real keywords may share less.
We followed up the question section because it passed on our first test's queries, so estimates that include them may run high; the 180 new queries avoid this but are longer, and the gain rose with query length: at the first test's query lengths it is $+0.033$.
An earlier sensitivity test missed its preset bar: a one-sentence answer raised the page's citation share by 0.007 to 0.034, not the 0.10 required; our judges come from its exploratory part.
The tests cover 320 English queries, nearly all edited by one model, from the same family as one of the judges.
The planned byte check of the harness prompt against the bench's has not been run.
Next are a comparison with live engines (a planned Gemini-judge check stalled on API quota) and a study with writers.
