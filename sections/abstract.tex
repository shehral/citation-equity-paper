\begin{abstract}
Writers are told that question headings, lists, statistics, quotes and cited sources help AI answer engines cite their pages.
% src: ten tool checks and one candidate of ours: CE/experiments/PREREG_E1.md lines 50 to 52 (family A = the tool's checks, family
%      B = candidates; the one that helped joined the tool, CE/docs/evidence.md line 3); one change at a time against a paraphrase
%      of the same spans (same length for rewrites; nothing inserted for the additive checks, PREREG_E1.md line 75), open models
%      over fixed sources: CE/experiments/PREREG_E1.md (design) and CE/experiments/RESULTS_E1.md; data/e1_results.json
We tested ten such checks and one of our own by editing real web pages one change at a time, each against a paraphrase of the same spans or the unedited page, while open models answered from fixed sources.
% src: "about 0.03": E1 target questions answered (question section) +0.027 and your reader's words up top +0.035 (data/e1_results.json
%      "main" arms 9 and 12; CE/experiments/RESULTS_E1.md); the 0-to-1 citation score (5 - r)/4: CE/experiments/PREREG_E1.md line 119
Only one kind of single change clearly raised citation: the reader's own words near the top of the page, by about 0.03 on a 0-to-1 citation score.
% src: "single change": all fixes together, a combination, also helped (data/e1_results.json main ALL vs original +0.049; Figure 1a)
% src: heading vs the same text as plain text +0.003 (replication -0.001), "no effect larger than delta": CE/experiments/RESULTS_E2.md
%      (data/e1_results.json "e2"."e2a"); question headings, cited sources, direct answer first over 260 queries (E3 pooled) and lists
%      over 80 (E1): "no effect larger than delta" with delta = 0.03: CE/experiments/RESULTS_E3.md, RESULTS_E1.md (data/e1_results.json)
The heading form did not matter.
Question headings, lists, cited sources and a direct answer first showed no effect larger than 0.03.
% src: E5 (CE/experiments/RESULTS_E5.md; data/e5_results.json): 180 new main queries (queries.new), of which the edit applied and
%      passed on 115 (so "queries the first test never used", no count: review demo:F09); the exact question vs the unedited page
%      on the new queries d +0.039, n 115, "helps, possibly through length or query-term overlap" (contrast
%      "main:9-original:new queries"); a keyword's two words kept 0.65 of the gain on the new queries (share_kept_new.9K) and helps
%      against both references (+0.016, +0.018) but falls short of the exact question (the plan's "loses the gain":
%      readings.retention.9K), so "helped, but less"; a question a local model wrote from the keyword: "no effect larger than
%      delta", d +0.008
On queries the first test never used, the gain held ($+0.039$).
A keyword's two words helped, but less.
A question a local model wrote from that keyword showed no effect larger than 0.03.

We present Citation Equity, an open-source writing tool rebuilt around this result.
It starts from the questions readers ask, shows which of their words a draft lacks near the top, flags settings that keep a page out of answers, and labels the other advice with what our tests found.
It runs in the browser and offline.
\end{abstract}
