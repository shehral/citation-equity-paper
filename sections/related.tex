The bench reuses GEO's prompt and word share~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}; our tests follow C-SEO Bench and What Gets Cited, which edit one page and hold the rest fixed~\cite{puerto2025cseo,vishwakarma2026cited}.
% src: GEO's Keyword Stuffing ("Modifies content to include more keywords from the query, as expected in classical SEO
%      optimization"): 17.7 vs 19.3 at baseline, "little to no improvement"; it added keywords anywhere in the page, not into the
%      title and opening (CE/docs/evidence.md line 298; CE/docs/research/evidence-notes.md lines 15 and 162). No test here varied
%      placement, so the text does not say placement caused the gain (review pc-10).
Our vocabulary finding fits earlier ones: relevance edits moved models and stylistic edits did not~\cite{wan2024conflictingqa}, and a page holding the query's terms won the first citation in four of six models~\cite{vishwakarma2026cited}.
GEO's keyword stuffing, a rewrite that added query keywords anywhere in the page, did not help~\cite{aggarwal2024geo}; our edit adds only the missing query words, to the title and opening, against a same-length placebo.

%      tested on their own"); all fixes together: data/e1_results.json main ALL vs original +0.049, helps (Figure 1a); the

A preprint that restructured 200 articles (headings, chunking, lists and tables at once) reports more citations across six engines~\cite{yu2026geosfe}; all our fixes together also helped (Figure~\ref{fig:result}a), but one change at a time only the vocabulary edits did.
