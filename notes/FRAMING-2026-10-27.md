# Framing for the WWW 2027 demo paper

Written 2026-10-27. These are the owner's choices:
- **Hook:** the myth-check. Most GEO writing advice did not move citation; one free change did.
- **Name:** keep "Citation Equity:" and change the subtitle.
- **Voice:** plain and crisp.
- **Main audience:** IR and web researchers.

This note is the target for the Week 10 rewrite (see REDESIGN-AND-E5-PLAN-2026-10-27.md). Nothing in the paper has changed yet.

## Title

**Recommended:** Citation Equity: Testing GEO Writing Advice, and Keeping Only What Moved Citation

Alternatives, in the same voice:
- Citation Equity: Which GEO Writing Advice Moves Citation? A Tool Built From the Answer
- Citation Equity: A Writing Tool That Tested Eleven Pieces of GEO Advice and Kept One

Why: the current title ("A Writing Tool for AI Search That Tests Its Own Advice") leads with the artifact and hides the finding. WWW reviewers know "GEO" from Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024), so the term needs no gloss in the title.

## The one-sentence claim

Of eleven widely given pieces of GEO writing advice, only putting the reader's own words near the top of a page clearly raised citation, and we built an open writing tool around that result.

Every section should support this sentence, and nothing should compete with it.

## Abstract draft (about 150 words; post-redesign)

> Writers are told that question headings, lists, statistics, quotes and cited sources help AI answer engines cite their pages. We tested eleven such checks by editing real web pages one change at a time. Each edit was compared with a same-length paraphrase of the same passage, and open models answered from fixed sources. Only one kind of change clearly raised citation: putting the reader's own words near the top of the page (about +0.03 on a 0-to-1 citation score, stable across editor and judge models). The heading form did not matter; the words did. Question headings, lists, cited sources and leading with a direct answer showed no effect larger than 0.03. [E5 sentence, if it lands: the gain held / shrank when the question was guessed from a keyword.] We present Citation Equity, an open-source writing tool rebuilt around this result. It starts from the questions readers ask and shows which of their words a draft is missing near the top. It also flags settings that keep a page out of answers, and keeps the other advice as unscored notes labelled with what our tests found. It runs in the browser and offline.

**Fallback if the redesign build slips:** replace the last three sentences with "The tool scores what helped, marks the rest as unscored notes, and runs in the browser and offline."

**Moved out of the abstract:**

## Introduction, four paragraphs

1. **Stakes.** Answer engines now choose which pages readers see cited. One field experiment found that an AI-only results page cut click-through by 18.8 points. Writers respond with GEO advice and products.
2. **The gap.** The advice is mostly untested:
   - the headline "up to 40%" gain comes from one setup with five sources;
   - a 45-study survey rejects it as a general claim;
   - adding statistics lowered rank in 19 of 24 C-SEO Bench settings.
   Most products sell brand tracking or rewrite the text.
3. **What we did and found (Figure 1).** Eleven checks, each tested one at a time against a paraphrase placebo, on 80 GEO-bench queries with a 60-query C-SEO Bench replication. Plans were committed before the runs. Only the reader's-words change helped. E2 shows that the words carry the gain, not the heading.
4. **What we built and how to see it.** The redesigned tool, the booth loop, the links.

The contributions, stated once at the end of the introduction:
1. a controlled, planned-in-advance test of eleven common GEO checks, with placebos, a replication set and editor and judge swaps;
2. the finding that vocabulary, not formatting, moved citation;
3. an open, offline writing tool built from that finding, with a live demo, code and data.

## What IR and web reviewers will ask, and where the paper answers

| Question | Answer to put in the text |
|---|---|
| "Open models over fixed sources are not ChatGPT." | Chosen for control and reproducibility; drift in live engines is cited; stated in the abstract and in Limitations. |
| "Isn't this just lexical overlap with the query?" | Yes, and that is the point for writers: the formatting they are sold did not matter, while matching the reader's vocabulary did. It is consistent with retrieval-style matching. Say it plainly rather than as a caveat. |
| "0.03 is tiny." | Put it next to the "up to 40%" claims it tests. It is about one rank position in eight answers. The other checks show no effect even at this size. |
| "Many tests, some will pass by chance." | Holm correction within families; plans committed before the runs, with timestamps. |
| "Did the edit change length?" | Each edit is compared with a same-length paraphrase; the length ratios are reported. |
| "Is it the editor model?" | E2b re-edited the pages with another model family, and the sign held. |

## Voice rules for the rewrite

- One claim per sentence. Lead with the takeaway, then the number.
- Numbers only where they carry the point: about 6 in the abstract, not 12.
- No defensive hedges in the abstract or introduction. Limitations get their own short paragraph and are stated once.
- Captions open with a bold takeaway sentence.
- Use the tool's check names everywhere, never arm numbers.
- No em dashes. Never "contradicted".

## The name

"Citation Equity" stays. One clause in the introduction says what it means: free, open and offline, so the evidence is not reserved for brands that pay for tracking tools. The paper makes no fairness claim beyond that.
