# Reporting the second-rater result for Figure 3

## Procedure

1. Send `rating_sheet.csv` and `INSTRUCTIONS.md` only (nothing else from this repo) to the second rater (Ishan or Akshay). They return a filled copy.
2. Score it: `python3 -I -B scripts/rater_agreement.py score <their_sheet>.csv`. This prints agreement and Cohen's kappa and writes `notes/second-rater/agreement.json`.
3. Use the sheet as built. It was generated by `make-sheet` with seed 20261026 (the same cells come back from the same coding; `--force` is needed to regenerate, and regenerating after a change to `data/evidence_matrix.csv` changes the sample).
4. Do not look at the disagreements until you have decided what to do with them (below).

## Numbers to report

- **Headline: kappa on the full ten-label set**, with percent agreement (`full_set`).
- **Collapsed set** (positive / null / negative / other, where other = survey, background, empty): less sensitive to the fine glyph distinctions (`collapsed_set`). `gain_part` counts as positive.
- Also in the json: kappa on the 37 coded cells only (`coded_cells_only_full_set`), and whether the rater found a finding where we left the cell empty (`cell_present_vs_empty`). Half the sample is empty cells by design, which makes the headline kappa look better than agreement on the substantive cells alone. Quote the coded-cells-only figure beside the headline.
- Rule of thumb for wording: kappa above 0.8 is "almost perfect", 0.6 to 0.8 "substantial", 0.4 to 0.6 "moderate" (Landis and Koch). Do not call a low value good.

## Caption wording (Figure 3)

Final form, after resolution:

> Cells were coded by two raters from the studies themselves (the second rater blind to the first rater's coding, on a sample of 74 of the 99 cells: all 37 coded cells and 37 randomly chosen empty cells). Agreement was X% (Cohen's kappa Y; Z% and kappa W after merging the labels into positive, null, negative and other). The N disagreements were resolved by discussion; the figure shows the resolved coding.

If you cannot resolve by discussion or choose to keep the first coding: replace the last clause with "the N disagreements are listed in the supplement and the figure shows the first rater's coding". Report the pre-discussion numbers either way; never report agreement after discussion as agreement.

Also add one sentence to the data/README.md "second rater" list and to the limitations paragraph that mentions single-rater coding (Section 2.2), giving the same numbers.

## What to do with disagreements

1. Sort them by `collapsed_ours` versus `collapsed_rater`. A disagreement that vanishes in the collapsed set (for example controlled_gain vs indirect_gain) is a judgement about directness; one that survives (for example null vs gain_part) is a reading of the paper.
2. Both raters reopen the cited place in the study, read it together, and agree on one label. Write the reason in one line.
3. If the reason is a rule (for example "a formatting-only null counts as null for lists"), state the rule in `data/README.md` and apply it to every cell, not only the disputed one. Then check that the rest of the coding follows the rule.
4. If the resolved label changes a cell in `data/evidence_matrix.csv`, edit the row (and its `note`), then rerun `derive_loose_grades.py` and `fig_evidence.py`. Check whether any strict or looser grade changes and report that in the paper; a changed shipped grade is a finding for the tool repo too.
5. Rater found a finding in a cell we coded empty (the checks and studies pairs outside the registry's sources): add the row with a `source_line`, or document why not. The script that derives the grades requires every coded study to appear in the registry's sources, so a new cell also needs the tool repo's evidence page updated first.
6. Keep the filled sheet, `agreement.json` and a short resolution log (one line per disagreement) in `notes/second-rater/`.
7. If kappa on the full set is below 0.6, do not just resolve and move on. Read the disagreements for a pattern (a systematic directness rule), fix the rule in the instructions, and consider a third rating of the affected cells.

## Limits to state

Two raters on 74 of 99 cells; the sample is balanced (half empty), so kappa is not the population value; both raters know the project; the second rater read the studies, not our summaries, but the where-to-look pointers come from our reading.
